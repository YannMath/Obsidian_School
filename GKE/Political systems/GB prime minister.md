<h2>Role</h2>

The British prime minister acts as the *head of government* in the [[Political systems - Great Britain|political system of Great Britain]].

---

<h2>Relationship to legislative & executive</h2>

> - is responsible to parliament
> - vote-of-no-confidence possible
> - member of House of Commons
> - chief of executive

In the [[Political systems - Great Britain|British political system]] the prime minister is *responsible to parliament*. He *forms the House of Commons together with the Cabinet*. There is a *vote-of-no-confidence possible*.

He is the *chief of executive* which means he *mainly focuses on work* but in addition *takes part in ceremonies*.

--- 

<h2>Relationship to the cabinet & other parties</h2>

> - has to lead a party
> - forms House of Commons together with the Cabinet

In comparison to the [[US president]] or the [[German chancellor]] the British prime minister *has to be a party leader* as the leader of the party that wins the election automatically becomes the prime minister.

In Great Britain the *Cabinet plays a key role* in the government. Together with the prime minister the Cabinet *forms the House of Commons*.

---

<h2>Ability to achieve desired policies</h2>

> - very likely to succeed with his plans

For the British prime minister passing new laws is *extremely likely to succeed* as he *always has a majority* in the House of Commons which makes the law-making process easier for him.

---

<h2>Length of term</h2>

> 5 years

The british prime minister has a term of *5 years* although he *might not see out the full term* as he *can call for early elections* or *his position within the party might change* not making him the party anymore. He *can be reelected* as long as he stays the party leader.
